## Context

The company manufactured a wide range of construction products: joists,
blocks, filler blocks and many more. Production was stored in about **128
quadrants** along **three internal lanes**, similar to supermarket aisles.
Customers picked up goods in scheduled slots, and operators loaded the trucks
with an overhead crane.

## The problem

The problem had two parts. On one side, trucks could wait whole days, sometimes
up to three, to be served. On the other, goods were not placed according to
demand, so the crane traveled long distances and loading took a long time.

## What we did

I was part of the team from the analysis stage.

- **Turnover analysis.** Using the sales history in Excel, we ran an ABC
  classification (Pareto curve): A products, with high turnover, accounted for
  most pickups; B products had medium turnover and C products, low.
- **Storage by turnover.** Each class got its own zone: high-turnover products were stored near the entrance
  and the main lanes; medium-turnover products in an intermediate area; and
  low-turnover products in the farthest sectors.

Then **I built the production planning system**:

- **Producing while knowing where to store.** Since pickup slots were booked
  three months ahead, the system knew what had to be produced. When production
  was recorded, it suggested the quadrant with available space that would let
  the customer pick it up fastest on their slot.
- **Stock per quadrant and per row.** If a quadrant held four rows of joists and
  a customer took one, that row was deducted and the quadrant stayed occupied
  with the other three; once empty, it was freed.
- **Loading plan.** When each truck arrived for its slot, the system gave the operator a plan
  stating which quadrant to pick each product from and in what order.

## Results

- **Truck waiting time dropped to zero:** trucks arriving during their slot were
  served right away.
- **Shorter loading time inside the plant**, because goods were already stored
  in the most convenient quadrants.
